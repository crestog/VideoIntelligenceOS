Setup

Everything the capture engine needs, in one form. Values live in memory for as long as this process runs — nothing here is written to a file or committed to the repository, so on a fresh Kaggle session you paste them again. The Help tab explains where to get each one.

Telegram — where the reels are stored

The API ID and hash are only needed for two things: reading the channel's history to find reels you already uploaded, and uploading a file larger than 50 MB. Capture works without them.

Instagram — proving you are logged in

Cookies

Saved and private reels are only visible to a logged-in account, so the downloader needs your session cookie. Use a second, throwaway Instagram account — if anything ever goes wrong it is that account that pays for it, not your main one. Export in Netscape format and paste the whole file below.

Pace — how invisible you want to be

Careful keeps the request rate sixty times under the level where Instagram starts refusing, and shapes the gaps so there is no rhythm to detect.

Import — what to capture

Your saved reels

Drop in the Instagram data export (.zip) or a markdown file of links. Importing the same file twice is safe — anything already known is counted as a duplicate and left alone.

Already uploaded

Reels that are in the channel but not in the ledger. Scanning the channel finds them automatically; pasting links is the fallback when the API ID and hash are not available.

Ledger — the thing that must never be lost

The ledger is what remembers, months from now, that a reel was already captured. It is uploaded to the channel and pinned every 25 reels, so a Kaggle session dying costs you nothing.

Asset sets — what makes Atlas play instantly

Every reel captured from now on gets cut into two-second clips and uploaded under its own message, with a manifest listing them. Atlas plays a clip instead of seeking into a 20 MB file, which is the difference between a second and half a minute. Reels captured before this existed have no clips — this builds them, one video at a time, without touching Instagram: the bytes come back out of the channel. It is safe to leave running and safe to stop; the ledger remembers exactly which videos are done. A session arms this by itself about a minute after boot, so the button is there for a second pass rather than for the first one.

How this works

Read once, set up in about ten minutes, then leave it running for a week.

The idea

Instagram is not an archive. Reels get deleted, accounts go private, and a collection you spent two years building can disappear in an afternoon. This tool copies every reel you saved — the original video file, the caption, the view and like counts, and as many comments as Instagram will hand over — into a Telegram channel that has no size limit and no expiry.

It does this slowly and on purpose. About one reel every two minutes, with irregular gaps, occasional long breaks and a quiet period overnight. That is roughly 0.5 requests per minute against a threshold that starts biting around 30. Downloading five thousand reels takes about a week. Downloading them in an afternoon gets the account checkpointed and gets you nothing.

It never downloads the same reel twice. Every capture is written to a ledger before the next one starts, and the ledger is backed up into the channel itself. Stop after two hours, come back in three months on a different machine, restore the ledger, and it picks up exactly where it left off.

Step by step

01 Make a Telegram channel and a bot
  1. In Telegram, create a new private channel. This is where every reel will live, so give it a name you will recognise in five years.
  2. Open a chat with @BotFather, send /newbot, and follow the two prompts. It replies with a token that looks like 1234567890:AAH… — that is your bot token.
  3. Go to your channel → Administrators → Add admin, search for your bot by its username, and add it. Give it Post messages and Delete messages. Without Post it cannot upload anything; without Delete the channel scan leaves a stray dot behind each time.
  4. To find the channel ID: forward any message from the channel to @userinfobot, or open the channel in Telegram Web and read it out of the URL. It is a negative number starting -100.
Treat the bot token like a password. Anyone who has it can post to and read your channel. If it has ever been pasted somewhere public, send /revoke to BotFather and use the new one.
02 Get an API ID and hash — optional

Skip this if you only want to capture. You need it for exactly two things: reading the channel's history (so reels you uploaded before today are not downloaded again) and uploading files over 50 MB, which almost no reel is.

  1. Go to my.telegram.org and log in with your phone number.
  2. Open API development tools, fill in any app name and short name.
  3. Copy the App api_id (a number) and the App api_hash (32 hex characters).
These identify you personally, not a bot. They cannot be revoked as easily as a bot token. Never put them in a file, a notebook cell or a screenshot — paste them into the form on the Setup tab and nowhere else.
03 Make a throwaway Instagram account

The downloader signs in as you to see your saved reels. Use a second account for this, never your real one.

  1. Create a new Instagram account. Give it a profile picture and follow twenty or thirty accounts — a completely empty account that immediately starts fetching reels looks exactly like what it is.
  2. Let it sit for a day or two before using it here.
  3. If your saved reels are on your main account, you have two choices: export the links from the main account (step 4) and let the throwaway do the downloading, which works for any public reel; or accept the risk and use the main account's cookies for private ones.
04 Export your saved reels from Instagram
  1. In the Instagram app: Settings → Accounts Centre → Your information and permissions → Download your information.
  2. Choose Some of your information and tick Saved (and Likes if you want those too). Pick JSON, not HTML.
  3. Choose the smallest date range that covers what you want, and request the download. It arrives by email in anything from a few minutes to a day.
  4. Upload the .zip straight into the Import box on the Setup tab. There is no need to unpack it.
The importer reads the whole export looking for Instagram links, rather than expecting one exact file layout — so it keeps working when Meta renames things, which they do. Collection names are picked up automatically.

If you already keep a list of links somewhere, a markdown file works just as well:

## Business
https://www.instagram.com/reel/ABC123/
https://www.instagram.com/reel/DEF456/
## Fitness
https://www.instagram.com/reel/GHI789/

05 Export your cookies
  1. Log into Instagram in a browser, using the throwaway account.
  2. Install a cookie exporter extension — Get cookies.txt LOCALLY for Chrome, or cookies.txt for Firefox. Pick one that works offline; an extension that uploads your cookies to a server is handing over the account.
  3. With instagram.com open, click the extension and export in Netscape format.
  4. Open the downloaded file in a text editor, copy everything, and paste it into the Cookies box on the Setup tab.
Cookies expire. Instagram sessions last weeks, not months. If the run starts failing with login errors, export them again — the ledger keeps everything it already captured, so refreshing costs nothing but a minute.
06 Check, then start
  1. Go to the Run tab and press Run pre-flight check. It verifies the downloader is installed, the bot can actually post to the channel, the cookies are loaded, there is disk space, and there is something queued.
  2. Press Scan channel for existing reels once, if you have uploaded reels before. It reads the channel and marks everything it finds as already captured.
  3. Press Start capturing and leave it. The status bar shows what it is doing and when the next reel is due.
On Kaggle, a session runs for at most 12 hours. That is fine — start it, come back, start it again. Every reel is committed before the next one begins, and the ledger is pinned in your channel every 25 reels. A new session only needs Restore from Telegram before it starts.

When things go wrong

“Instagram pushed back”

A rate limit or a login wall. The engine slows itself down automatically and retries that reel in two hours. If it happens five times in a row it stops on purpose rather than digging the hole deeper — refresh the cookies, wait a few hours, and start again.

“Gone from Instagram”

The post was deleted or the account went private. Nothing to fix; these are recorded so you know what was lost and are never retried.

The bot cannot post

Almost always the bot was added to the channel but not given Post messages. Channel → Administrators → your bot → turn it on. The pre-flight check tells you which of the two it is.

The session died mid-reel

Expected. That reel's row is left mid-flight and repaired on the next start, which puts it back in the queue with its attempt counted. Nothing else is affected.

It says everything is captured but the channel looks empty

The ledger and the channel have drifted — usually from restoring an old ledger over a newer one. Press Rescan from the beginning on the Setup tab; it rebuilds the ledger from what is actually in the channel, which is the real source of truth.

What gets stored

For each reel, two messages go into the channel:

  • The original video, sent as a video so Telegram also stores a thumbnail and plays it inline, captioned with the collection, creator, view and like counts, the description, and the permalink.
  • A metadata record attached as a reply — a JSON file holding everything above plus the comments, the full untouched response from Instagram, file hashes, and which tool captured it and when.

The raw response is kept deliberately. Anything useful in it that nobody has thought to use yet is still there in two years, without re-downloading a single reel — by which time some of them will no longer exist.
